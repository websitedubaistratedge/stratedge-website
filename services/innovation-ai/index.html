Where should a business actually start with AI?

With a problem worth solving, not with the technology. The useful first question is which decisions or processes are expensive, repetitive and data-rich — those are where AI changes the economics. Starting from the tool rather than the problem is how pilots stall.

Why do AI pilots so rarely reach production?

Because the pilot proved the model worked, not that the business could run it. Production needs data quality, ownership, workflow change and a measure of success. Those are organisational questions, and they are usually the ones skipped.

Do we need our data in order before starting?

Not entirely, but you need to know its state. Data analytics work establishes what you actually hold, how reliable it is, and what has to be true before a given use case is realistic. That assessment often reorders the roadmap.

What the work covers

The work begins with an honest assessment of where technology would change something material, and where it would only add cost. Emerging technology insight establishes what is genuinely available and proven rather than what is being marketed.

From there, candidate use cases are identified and put through a business case: what it changes, what it costs, what has to be true, and how it would be measured. Data analytics establishes whether the underlying information supports the ambition. Digital transformation strategy sets the sequence, and the innovation roadmap makes it a plan with owners rather than a list of possibilities.

Where the honest answer is that a use case is not yet worth pursuing, the engagement says so. That finding, delivered before the spend, is often the most valuable output.

Innovation is a sequencing problem

Most organisations do not lack ideas. They lack an order — a defensible view of which three things to do first given the capital, the data and the capacity available.

A roadmap that ranks opportunities against readiness rather than excitement is what turns innovation from an initiative into a capability.
